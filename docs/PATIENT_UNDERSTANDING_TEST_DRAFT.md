# Patient-understanding check — draft protocol and score sheet

**Status:** Preparation draft only, 25 September 2026. Do not recruit or run sessions from this draft until the project owner has checked event/institution requirements, an appropriate reviewer has approved the materials and answer keys, and a consent, privacy, retention, and stop procedure has been agreed. This is a small usability exercise, not clinical research evidence or proof of effectiveness.

## Purpose and limits

Compare how well five people can find three facts in an English-only fictional visit summary versus a Khabar summary in a language they choose. The exercise checks whether the information is understandable; it does not evaluate clinical accuracy, health outcomes, adherence, or emergency detection.

Use only fictional cases and reviewer-approved summaries. Do not ask participants about their own health, medicines, diagnoses, or experiences. Do not use real patient records. Do not record audio or video. Do not collect names, contact information, age, or diagnoses on the score sheet. A participant code is still pseudonymous data; decide who can access the sheets, where they are stored, how long they are retained, and when they are destroyed before recruiting anyone.

## Materials to approve before a session

- Two fictional cases, A and B, matched for reading difficulty and containing a medicine, when to take it, a dose, and one warning symptom with a clear next step.
- For each case, an English-only summary and a Khabar summary in the target language. A qualified clinician must approve the medical content and answer key; a fluent reader must review each translation.
- A facilitator script and score sheet below, checked against the final summaries and any event or institutional requirements.
- A documented consent/withdrawal explanation, privacy and retention plan, and a stop procedure. This file is not a consent form.

## Assignment and procedure

1. Recruit five volunteers only after approval. Assign session codes `P01`–`P05`; do not maintain an identity-to-code key unless an approved process requires one.
2. Counterbalance case and format: three participants see Case A in English and Case B in Khabar; two see Case A in Khabar and Case B in English. Randomly choose which three get the first mapping. Randomize whether A or B is shown first where practical and record the presentation order separately.
3. At the start, read the approved introduction. Tell the participant the cases are fictional, this tests the wording rather than the participant, participation is voluntary, and they can stop without giving a reason. Do not imply that the prototype is for their care.
4. Show one summary at a time. Do not explain or correct it. Ask the same three questions below and record correct, incorrect, or skipped using the approved answer key. Then repeat for the other case/format.
5. After both summaries, ask: “Which summary was easier to understand, and what made it easier or harder?” Record only a short non-identifying theme if the reviewer-approved privacy plan permits it; do not record names or verbatim personal disclosures.
6. If a participant discusses personal health or becomes uncomfortable, stop the session, do not enter that information in the sheet, and follow the approved stop/withdrawal process.

## Facilitator script

> Thanks for helping us check whether these example summaries are easy to understand. The people and cases are fictional. We are testing the wording, not you. This is not medical advice and this tool is not for your care. You may skip a question or stop at any time. Please do not share your own health information. I will show one summary at a time and ask the same questions about each. I cannot explain the summary while you answer, but I can repeat a question.

Ask exactly these questions for each summary:

1. Which medicine is mentioned, and when should it be taken?
2. What dose does the summary say?
3. Which warning symptom is mentioned, and what next step does the summary give?

Use the same approved answer key to score both formats. Do not prompt for missing information or count a guess as correct unless the key's pre-approved scoring rule says so.

## Summary template (fill only after all five approved sessions)

- Participants completed: `__/5` (report withdrawals/skips without identifying anyone).
- English-only correct responses: `__/15` across medicine/timing, dose, and warning/next-step questions.
- Khabar correct responses: `__/15` across the same questions.
- For each participant, Khabar total minus English-only total: record `-3` to `+3`, using the approved answer key.
- Common wording difficulties: `________________` (aggregate themes only; no identifying quotations).
- Deviations, skipped questions, or stop events: `________________` (describe without identifying details).

Report counts and limitations plainly. Five volunteers are a usability signal only; do not generalize to a population, compare languages with such a small sample, or claim clinical benefit. If a summary is medically wrong or a warning is misunderstood, pause and refer the content to the clinician reviewer before any further sessions.
